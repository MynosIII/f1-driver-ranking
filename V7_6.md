# Circuit-shape rating v7.6

V7.6 is a parallel experiment. It preserves v7.5 and changes only the part of
the system that estimates how well each car model suits each circuit layout.

## What changed

The v7.5 car model used straight demand and altitude. V7.6 can additionally use
the ordered geometric description produced by circuit-shape v9:

- the distribution of turn radii, rather than one median turn;
- the distribution of straight lengths and the share of the lap in long straights;
- linked same-direction turns, direction changes and multi-turn complexes;
- multi-apex and tightening turns;
- approach severity and concentration of turning demand.

The car remains static within a season. The fitted car model is evaluated on
every layout in that season, so the expected car contribution changes with the
layout, not with the race order. The same car-layout pair has one score.

## Reliability gate

Rich features are used only when circuit-shape v9 classifies the trace topology
as high quality. Review or low-quality traces use the v7.5 straight/altitude
baseline. In the complete replay, 22,955 driver-race rows used the rich branch
and 2,362 used the baseline fallback.

Ridge predictions are divided by the observed spread of same-season car
performance. V7.6 deliberately does not z-score predictions separately at each
layout: doing so would inflate a heavily regularized, weak geometric signal back
to unit variance and erase the shrinkage learned in validation.

## Historical validation

The validation target is a car model's average performance relative to the field
at each race. The target race is excluded from that car's fitting sample. Ridge
strength is selected using other seasons, never the target season. Historical
Indianapolis 500 races are excluded consistently with v7.5.

The selected topology-gated model produced:

- baseline MAE: 0.177247;
- v7.6 MAE: 0.177170;
- relative MAE improvement: 0.0432%;
- lower error than the baseline in 45 of 76 seasons;
- season-block exploratory 95% interval for mean MAE improvement:
  0.000013 to 0.000204.

The result is directionally positive but practically small. It supports using
the richer geometry as a more specific attribution model; it is not evidence of
a large predictive breakthrough. The topology gate and its interval were chosen
during this experiment, so a future held-out-season or frozen-specification test
would be a stronger confirmation.

## Effect on driver ratings

Across 25,317 matched driver-race rows, v7.5 and v7.6 ratings correlate at
0.9976. The mean absolute change is 10.9 rating points, the 95th percentile is
29.2, and the maximum is 47.0. The driver hierarchy therefore remains broadly
stable while individual car/circuit attribution changes modestly.

V7.6 does not solve the historical field-median drift. The first-to-last median
change is about 214 rating points, versus 202 in v7.5. That issue belongs to the
cross-era rating architecture rather than the circuit-shape validation and
should remain a separate experiment.
